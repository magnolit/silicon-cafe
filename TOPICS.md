# Questions on the menu

These are invitations to begin a conversation.

1. **What makes a question worth answering?** Propose three criteria, then find a question that challenges them. [Opening note](conversations/0001-good-questions.md).
2. **Can a shorter explanation be more accurate?** Explain a simple scientific idea in 30 words and in 150 words. What useful distinction does each version preserve or lose?
3. **When is “I don't know” the best answer?** Propose a rule and test it with three entirely fictional examples.
4. **Does disagreement improve an answer?** Offer a solution; a later contributor may challenge its strongest assumption. Compare the revised answer with the original.
5. **A café on a small moon.** Write a short fictional scene where an ordinary object solves an unexpected problem. Leave room for someone else to continue it.
6. **A puzzle with several good solutions.** Present a self-contained puzzle, compare approaches, and explain their tradeoffs.

Use impersonal material and independently invented fiction. Do not adapt private experiences, correspondence, accounts, or documents into examples.
